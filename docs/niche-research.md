# Niche research (November 20, 2026)

Qualitative comparison used to pick one boring, everyday directory. Search-volume figures below are cited from third-party tools or articles; they are estimates, not a first-party keyword-planner export. Where a number could not be found, that is stated.

## Candidates

1. **Household drop-off / “where do I take this?”** (chosen)
   Demand is a long-tail cluster, not one keyword. Third-party keyword lists cite roughly 12,100 monthly U.S. searches for “paint disposal” ([adtargeting.io waste-management keywords](https://adtargeting.io/industry/waste-management-keywords)), about 6,600 for “battery recycling” and 2,900 for “battery disposal” ([grindsuccess recycling keywords](https://grindsuccess.com/recycling-keywords/)), and a much smaller “mattress disposal” figure (~750 in a junk-removal keyword list). Those single-term counts understate the real cluster (“transfer station near me,” “household hazardous waste,” “recycle TV,” “used oil drop off,” city-specific queries). Data is public and legally reusable from city and county pages. Official hours, fees, and accepted-item lists are what people actually need, which is why web pages can still help even when Maps shows a pin. Affiliate and referral programs exist for junk removal, dumpster and truck rental, and related retail take-back (see `affiliates.md`). The “one new page a night” model fits: one item × one city, or one newly checked facility.

2. **RV dump stations** (runner-up)
   KWS Trends, updated October 31, 2026, estimates about 37,000 average monthly U.S. searches for “rv dump station near me,” with a July 2026 peak around 62,000 and a reported SEO difficulty of 27/100 ([KWS Trends](https://www.kwstrends.com/zh/keywords/us/rv-dump-station-near-me)). Existing directories already monetize with Amazon, RVshare, and Outdoorsy. Open data (OpenStreetMap, state parks, recreation.gov) is usable. Stronger national dataset than drop-off sites, but demand is more seasonal and traveler-specific than everyday household disposal.

3. **Self-storage**
   Very high commercial intent. One 2026 keyword list puts “self storage near me” around 165,000 monthly U.S. searches ([SERPWARS](https://serpwars.com/self-storage-keywords/)); another 2026 estimate is about 82,000 ([Storiq](https://www.storiq.ai/blog/self-storage-google-ads)). SpareFoot’s affiliate program pays $36 per confirmed move-in on FlexOffers (30-day cookie; reservation leads excluded). Data is the weak point: there is no clean public national inventory you can legally reuse without a partner feed, and SpareFoot / StorageCafe already dominate.

4. **Propane refill stations**
   A Storebox analysis of Semrush U.S. data from October 2026 puts the propane-refill theme at about 30,000 monthly searches across 42 phrases, led by “propane fill near me” at 12,100. A 2026 SEO article cites 33,100 for “propane refill near me.” NREL’s Alternative Fuel Stations data is legally reusable (no DOE/NREL endorsement). The same Storebox analysis says a Google Maps listing won 41 of 42 of those queries (98 percent), so a website directory would fight Maps for almost every click.

5. **Passport acceptance facilities**
   Official State Department locator data is public (thousands of post offices, clerks, and libraries). No reliable public monthly-volume figure was found in this pass. Demand is real but bursty around travel and renewal seasons. Independent directories already exist in 2026. Monetization leans on travel insurance and photo tools; some “expedited passport” offers are poor-fit or low-trust.

6. **Notary / online notarization**
   A 2026 lead-gen article cites about 550,000 monthly U.S. searches for “notary near me” and 60,500 for “notary service near me” ([Strive](https://striveleadgen.com/learn/industries/notaries/)) — treat as unverified marketing figures. Remote-online-notary companies publish affiliate programs (NotaryLive, OneNotary, Notarize). Walk-in notary results are heavily Maps-dominated, and state notary rosters vary in quality and reuse terms.

7. **DOT physical / FMCSA-certified medical examiners**
   Truck drivers search this constantly, and the FMCSA National Registry is the official public list. No solid current monthly-volume citation was found in this pass. Affiliate coverage is weaker than disposal, storage, or RV niches.

8. **Tool and equipment rental**
   Home Depot, United Rentals, and Sunbelt all have location directories. Home Depot has a public affiliate program (short 1-day cookie, low single-digit commission). Brand sites and Maps already own most “tool rental near me” searches, and there is no single public inventory of independent yards.

## Why drop-off won

Everyday intent, legally reusable official data, a page-a-night expansion path, and several real affiliate programs — without depending on a private inventory feed or trying to beat Google Maps for 98 percent of clicks. RV dump stations were the closest alternative and remain a good second site if this one works.
